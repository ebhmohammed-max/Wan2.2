# The twelve argument motifs — how to decide what a panel *is*

Every one of the 46 graphic slots is an exhibit for one of these twelve ideas. Read the shot map's text for the slot,
decide which motif it serves, then build it with that motif's template and its prohibitions. If a slot's text does not
map onto any motif, that is a report line, not a licence to improvise.

Two standing rules for all twelve:
- **A panel states, it does not argue.** The narration argues. The panel gives the viewer the object, the name, the
  number, or the structure that the sentence is pointing at.
- **A panel never closes a question the film leaves open.** Where the film says *may*, the panel says *may*.

---

## M1 · The claim and its attribution
*"Alexandria's lighthouse appears on a bronze ring, according to its excavators."*
- Template: `CITE` (or `LOGIC` with one lane).
- Shows: the claim in the team's own framing, and the source line that makes it a report rather than a fact.
- May not: present the claim as established; quote an institution the script does not name; add a quote that was not
  spoken.

## M2 · Picture versus job — the film's spine
*"It can identify the subject of a picture. It cannot … identify the occupation of the person carrying it."*
- Template: `LOGIC`, two lanes: **WHAT THE IMAGE CAN DO** / **WHAT IT CANNOT**.
- Connectors: solid for the first lane, dashed `--rust` for the second.
- May not: use an icon of a person, a hand, a ring or a tower. Lanes are type + hairlines only.

## M3 · The object type: a signet is a seal
*"A signet can press an identifying design into a soft material. Think of a stamp carried with its user."*
- Template: `LOGIC` with three steps: **carry → press → mark travels**.
- May not: draw the seal, the impression, the wax, the moth, or any stamp-like motif. Words and arrows only.

## M4 · The museum comparison (gems and their uses)
*"Their impressions could establish ownership, authenticate documents, or protect private letters."*
- Template: `CITE` with a three-item list set as body copy, each on its own line, hairline-separated.
- May not: illustrate a document, a letter, a jar or a gem; imply the ring performed these functions.

## M5 · The catalogue record — certainty and uncertainty side by side
*"The museum says a signet ring may have produced those impressions … Neither identification is stated as certain."*
- Template: `FACT` (the accession block) plus `LOGIC` (a two-column split: *stated* / *may have been*).
- The word **may** survives verbatim in every rendering of this panel.
- May not: tidy the uncertainty away, add a confidence level, or rank the two possibilities.

## M6 · The earlier image — the novelty question
*"A copper-alloy coin minted in Alexandria, AD 133–134 … Here is an earlier lighthouse representation."*
- Template: `CITE` (typographic citation) — **there is no image of the coin in this film**.
- Shows: mint place, date, obverse/reverse description, institution. That is the whole panel.
- May not: draw a coin, a bust, a lighthouse, or a "reconstruction"; may not imply the coin is the find.

## M7 · Survey to site — how a feature becomes a record
*"Archives and ancient texts … multibeam bathymetry … side-scan sonar … test excavations … inventories, drawings,
photographs, and mapped records."*
- Template: `DIAGRAM` (VX-002 pipeline), five nodes, left to right.
- May not: show sonar, bathymetry, a seabed, a dive, a grid, or any site plan. The pipeline is a diagram of *method*,
  not of a place.

## M8 · Context, defined plainly
*"It is the object together with the things and structures around it."*
- Template: `LOGIC`: a small object block, a bracket, and the surrounding set as labelled type.
- May not: draw the object or the structures; "object" and "surroundings" are type blocks and a bracket.

## M9 · Two readings, not two owners
*"A reported administrative interpretation and a reported merchant alternative."*
- Template: `LOGIC`, two lanes of equal width and equal weight, both labelled **interpretation**.
- The lane heads are `--ivory`; only the word *interpretation* may take `--teal` (administrative) and `--amber`
  (merchant) — never a good/bad colour code.
- May not: present either lane as more likely; may not name a person as owner.

## M10 · Hypothesis versus result
*"A hypothesis is an explanation that needs testing … a possible answer, not the result."*
- Template: `LOGIC`: **hypothesis** (dashed outline) → **test** (solid arrow) → **result: not established** (`--rust`).
- May not: fill in a result, a probability, or a verdict beyond "not established".

## M11 · Association versus manufacture
*"An old keepsake beside a dated receipt. The receipt does not make the keepsake new."*
- Template: `DIAGRAM` (VX-003), two timelines with a shared "reported" bracket; earlier-object branch dashed `--rust`.
- The qualification is set verbatim, in the panel's own words from the report: *"also explicitly allows the ring to be
  earlier."*
- May not: draw the keepsake, the receipt, the pottery, or the ring; no dates that the script does not give.

## M12 · Three things kept distinct — the bounded answer
*"The reported palace find, the museum evidence for older lighthouse imagery, and the general practice of sealing."*
- Template: `LOGIC`, three columns, each with a hairline top rule and a one-line summary; a single bracket beneath
  reading **context — no named owner**.
- This is the film's closing structure. Build it once, well; it may recur at most twice.
- May not: merge the three, rank them, or add a fourth.
