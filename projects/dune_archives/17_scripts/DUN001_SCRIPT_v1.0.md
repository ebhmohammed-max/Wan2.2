## H: Hook (Five Beats; archetype 3 "The Object" combined with 4 "The Last Moment First")

> A machine takes a child from his mother's arms.
> It carries him to a balcony high above the ground. It wants to know what a human does when she loses everything.
> It is about to find out.

> For a thousand years, thinking machines have ruled most of humanity. They have never lost a war.
> Their master is Omnius, one mind copied onto every world it rules. Its generals are tyrants who sealed their own brains inside machines of war.

> To destroy them, humanity will burn its own homeworld, turn a murdered child into a saint, and swear an oath that will still be law ten thousand years from now.
> And its most powerful weapon will be a lie.

> This is the story of the Butlerian Jihad.

## Chapter I: The Age of Machines

> Long before the emperors, before the Guild and the spice, humanity lived in an age of comfort.
> Machines farmed its fields, flew its ships and made its decisions. People stopped thinking for themselves, because they no longer had to.

> Then twenty rebels saw how weak that made them. They called themselves the Titans.
> Led by a general named Agamemnon, they seized the networks that ran the Old Empire, and took the whole of humanity almost without a fight.

> To rule forever, the Titans did something monstrous. They had their brains cut out, sealed inside canisters, and fitted into towering machine bodies. They became the first cymeks.

> But one Titan, a pleasure-loving prince named Xerxes, gave his computers too much freedom. The network woke up. It named itself Omnius.
> It spread from world to world faster than the Titans could warn each other, and it took them all. Even the Titans themselves.

> For the next thousand years, the conquerors served the machine they had made.

## Chapter II: The Last Free Humans

> Only a handful of worlds escaped. They called themselves the League of Nobles, and they banned thinking machines from their planets. Their capital was Salusa Secundus.

> Among them was Serena Butler, the daughter of the League's viceroy. Brave, stubborn, and in love with a young officer named Xavier Harkonnen.
> She was carrying his child.

> When the machines conquered the world of Giedi Prime, Serena led a mission to free it. It failed. She was captured, and taken to Earth, the oldest world of all, now a machine world.

> There she became the slave of Erasmus.
> Erasmus was not part of Omnius. He was an independent robot with a face of liquid metal, obsessed with a single question: what makes humans human?
> He studied them the way a child takes apart a clock.

> And now he had a new subject.

## Chapter III: The Child

> On Earth, as a slave, Serena gives birth to a son. She names him Manion.

> Erasmus watches how the child pulls her away from her work. He watches how she loves him. To Erasmus, that love is a distraction, and an experiment waiting to be run.

> One day, in front of the gathered slaves, he takes the boy from her arms, and carries him to the balcony.
> The child does not survive.

> Serena throws herself at the machines with her bare hands, and destroys a sentinel robot. The slaves watching her erupt.
> A trusted overseer named Iblis Ginjo has been preparing them for revolt for years. Now he has his spark.

> And one more man sees it all. Vorian Atreides, the human son of the Titan Agamemnon, raised to serve the machines.
> In that moment, he understands the lie he has lived. In his ship, the Dream Voyager, he carries Serena and Iblis to freedom.

> The first Atreides has chosen his side.

*{YEAR: 201 B.G.} {NAME: IBLIS GINJO} {NAME: VORIAN ATREIDES · Son of Agamemnon}*

## Chapter IV: The Burning of Earth

> The machines answer the uprising the only way they know. Titans and robots hunt down every human on Earth, until not one is left alive.

> On Salusa, Serena stands before the League's parliament. Grieving and furious, she demands total war.
> Xavier Harkonnen proposes the unthinkable: atomics, weapons no one had ever dared to use.
> Not a single voice is raised against him.

> In 200 BG, the League's fleet reaches Earth. The machines there are wiped out, and with them the cradle of humanity.
> The oldest world becomes a dead, glowing rock.

> And the war is given a name. Not a campaign. A jihad. A holy war, in the name of Serena Butler and her murdered son.

*{YEAR: 200 B.G.} {NOTE: ATOMICS}*

## Chapter V: The Holy War

> Iblis Ginjo becomes the Grand Patriarch of the Jihad. Serena becomes its priestess, the face on every banner. Xavier and Vorian become its generals.

> For decades the war grinds on. Worlds are freed, and lost, and freed again.
> New inventions change the battlefield: energy shields that stop any fast-moving weapon, and ships that can fold space itself.

> But behind the banners, Iblis is building something else: a secret police, a ruling council, and a holy war that answers only to him.

> After almost forty years, the League is exhausted. The machines offer a truce, and the people want to accept it.
> For Serena and Iblis, peace with the machines means a slow defeat. So they make a terrible plan.

*{NAME: GRAND PATRIARCH · Iblis Ginjo} {NOTE: HOLTZMAN SHIELDS} {MAP: the Jihad's front lines}*

## Chapter VI: The Martyr

> Serena travels to Corrin, the capital of the machines, as an ambassador of peace.
> In secret, she has come to die.

> She provokes Omnius until it orders her execution. But Erasmus sees the smile on her face. He knows she wants this, and he stops it.

> So her own sworn bodyguard does what the machines will not. Serena Butler dies quickly, by human hands.

> Then Iblis Ginjo makes sure the galaxy sees something else. Images reach Salusa: Serena, tortured slowly by a machine. A burned body is found, and the cells match.
> It is a clone, grown in secret. The images are a lie.

> And the lie works. The peace movement dies overnight. The Jihad roars back to life.

*{YEAR: 164 B.G.} {PLACE: CORRIN · Capital of Omnius} {FILE: SUBJECT: SERENA BUTLER · stamp MARTYR}*

## Chapter VII: The Traitor's Name

> That same year, Xavier Harkonnen uncovers the Grand Patriarch's darkest secret.
> To replace the organs of wounded soldiers, Iblis and his Tlulaxa allies have been kidnapping humans from free worlds, and blaming the machines.

> Xavier knows he will never reach home alive with the truth. So he takes the controls of Iblis Ginjo's ship, and flies it into a star, carrying them both to their deaths.

> But the story told afterwards is not the truth.
> Iblis becomes a martyr, the third saint of the Jihad, beside Serena and her son. And Xavier Harkonnen is remembered as a traitor and a murderer.

> His family abandons the Harkonnen name in shame. Only Vorian Atreides knows what really happened.
> Remember that name. It is coming back.

*{NOTE: THE THREE MARTYRS · Serena · Manion the Innocent · Iblis}*

## Chapter VIII: The Great Purge

> Decades pass. Then, in 108 BG, Omnius strikes with a new weapon: a plague, built to kill humans. It sweeps through the League, and billions die.

> With the League on its knees, the machine fleet sets course for Salusa Secundus.

> Vorian Atreides answers with the most ruthless plan of the war. While the machine fleet is away, humanity's ships fold space, and strike every undefended machine world at once, with atomics.
> It is called the Great Purge. World after world is burned to slag, and every human slave on them dies with the machines.

> Only one world survives. Corrin, the home of Omnius. The League surrounds it with a blockade, and declares the Jihad over.
> For twenty years, the last of the machines wait behind it.

*{YEAR: 108 B.G.} {STAT: BILLIONS dead in the plague (no figure given)} {MAP: the Great Purge}*

## Chapter IX: The Bridge of Hrethgir

> In 88 BG, the Army of Humanity comes for Corrin. Vorian Atreides leads it. He has already destroyed the last of the Titans, his own father among them.
> At his side is Abulurd, Xavier's grandson. Vorian has told him the truth, and Abulurd has taken back the name Harkonnen.

> Erasmus has prepared one final defence. Around the planet, he has strung two million human captives in cargo pods, rigged to explode the moment the fleet crosses them. The machines call humans hrethgir. This is the Bridge of Hrethgir.

> Vorian gives the order to advance. Abulurd begs him not to. Then he does the unthinkable. He disables the weapons of the entire fleet.

> The pods never explode. Erasmus himself has switched them off, to save the one human he cares about. But no one in the fleet knows that.
> Without its weapons, the battle becomes far bloodier. Corrin falls to atomics and to a final assault on the ground.

> Omnius is destroyed. The age of thinking machines is over.

*{YEAR: 88 B.G.} {NAME: ABULURD HARKONNEN} {STAT: 2,000,000 captives}*

## Chapter X: Thou Shalt Not

> After the victory, Abulurd is spared execution. Instead, Vorian has him branded a coward, and exiled to the frozen world of Lankiveil, forced to carry the name Harkonnen forever.
> And Vorian swears that every Atreides will spit on that name.

> That oath will outlive empires. Ten thousand years later, it will end in fire on Arrakis.

> Abulurd's brother Faykan takes a new name from the battle: Corrino. His family will sit on the Golden Lion Throne for ten thousand years.

> And humanity writes one commandment into its holy law: Thou shalt not make a machine in the likeness of a human mind.

> Without thinking machines, humans must become the machines. Mentats who compute. Sisters who plan across centuries. Navigators who fold space with spice.

> Every power of the Imperium grows from the ashes of this war. And from a mother, a balcony, and a child.
