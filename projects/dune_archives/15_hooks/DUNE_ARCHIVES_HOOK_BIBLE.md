# THE DUNE ARCHIVES: Hook Bible (v1.0, 2026-11-24)

**Channel identity:** in-universe *Dune storytelling* documentaries. We tell the story as history. We are **not** a review, news, spoiler or film channel. **Never** open with release dates, cast, "before you watch", "explained" or book-vs-film framing.

---

## 1. What the research says (why hooks matter)

**Most viewers who leave, leave at the start.**
- YouTube's "Intro" metric is the share of viewers still watching after 30 s. A healthy target is 60–70 % or more. A strong hook holds 80–90 % ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/increase-audience-retention-youtube/), [PrePublish](https://prepublish.ai/guides/youtube-retention-drop-diagnosis)).
- 30–40 % of viewers leave most videos within the first 30 s ([mean.ceo summary of Miraflow data](https://blog.mean.ceo/video-hook-retention-statistics/)).

**Recommended hook type and length for documentary/story content:**
- The 2026 benchmarks put story content best served by a *question-driven* hook of **30–45 s**, with 55 %+ as the minimum 30-s retention ([TubeAnalytics](https://www.tubeanalytics.net/blog/youtube-video-hook-first-30-seconds)).

**Why openings fail:**
- The usual causes are a slow or branded cold open (logo, "welcome back") and a hook that doesn't match the title or thumbnail ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/increase-audience-retention-youtube/)).
- Bait-and-switch in the first 30 s causes sharp drop-offs. The hook must connect to the main narrative ([LongStories](https://longstories.ai/blog/long-form-video-storytelling-guide)).

**What works:**
- **Cold open, in medias res.** Start inside the action, and back-fill context once the viewer is hooked ([Van Pelt Studio](https://vanpelt.studio/storytelling-examples/)).
- **Open loops.** The mind treats an unanswered question as a debt and keeps watching. Close one loop only after opening the next ([Van Pelt Studio](https://vanpelt.studio/storytelling-examples/)).
- **Re-hooks at the seams.** Transitions and section changes are where viewers drift, so place a new hook there ([Gyre](https://gyre.pro/blog/how-to-improve-audience-retention-on-youtube-top-tips)). Plan a pattern interrupt every 30–60 s ([Isaacson](https://johnisaacson.co.uk/youtube-storytelling-framework/)).
- **Write the hook last.** Hooks written from the script tend to promise something the video only delivers at minute eight ([Gyre](https://gyre.pro/blog/how-to-improve-audience-retention-on-youtube-top-tips), [Rend](https://rend.ai/blog/video-essay-script-template)).
- **Narrator and writing for lore channels.**
  - Use a low, warm, measured voice at about 130–145 wpm, closer to a nature-documentary read than a hype voice.
  - Write short sentences, using present tense for in-universe events.
  - Put rhetorical questions before chapter breaks.
  
  ([SpeakSay](https://www.speaksay.com/blog/gaming-lore-youtube-channel-ai-narrator-playbook-2026))

## 2. The DUNE ARCHIVES hook formula: "THE FIVE BEATS" (35–45 s)

| Beat | Time | Job | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The Strike** | 0–6 s | A pattern interrupt | Start *in medias res* on the most charged image and line in the story. The first sentence is under 12 words, has a concrete subject, and is in present tense. |
| **2. The Contrast** | 6–15 s | Show the fall from height | Pair glory with ruin, e.g. *won everything, then 61 billion dead*. Use one hard number or one irreversible fact. |
| **3. The Threat** | 15–25 s | Stakes and an antagonist | Who wants this destroyed, and how? Name one strange, specific weapon (a gift, a dead man, a needle). |
| **4. The Twist / Open Loop** | 25–35 s | Plant the question that is only answered in the final act | Use dramatic irony when possible: *he can see the trap, and walks into it anyway.* |
| **5. The Title Drop** | 35–45 s | Make the promise and frame the story | "This is the story of…" goes over an animated title card. **No logo before this point.** |

**Always**
- Every line in the hook must be paid off later in the video. Keep a payoff checklist in the script header.
- Use the strongest image in the episode in the first 3 s. Picture and narration must match word for word.
- Bring the music in under the first word. Allow one silence or impact, but only one.
- Keep it to one idea per sentence. Cut "In this video", "Today we", "Let's dive in", "Before we begin", "Subscribe".

**Never**
- Open on dates, the cast, the films, release news, "explained" or "everything you need to know".
- Open with a channel logo or ident. The ident comes *after* the title drop, and lasts under 6 s.
- Open with a question the viewer can answer with "no" (e.g. "Have you ever wondered…?").
- Rely on vague superlatives ("the most insane story ever"). Be specific instead.

## 3. Ten hook archetypes for Dune storytelling (pick one per episode, and rotate them)

1. **The God Who Can See His Doom.** Use dramatic irony: the hero knows, and does it anyway. *"He can see the future. And in every future he sees, the galaxy burns."*
2. **The Number.** Lead with one devastating statistic, then make it human. *"Sixty-one billion dead. And the man responsible is the only one who knows exactly how many."*
3. **The Object.** Open on one object that carries the story (a crysknife, a gom jabbar, a ring). *"The needle is poisoned. The box is pain. The boy is fifteen."*
4. **The Last Moment First.** Show the ending, then rewind. *"There is no grave. No body. Just footprints the wind is already taking. To understand how he got here, we go back twelve years."*
5. **The Impossible Act.** *"A blind man walks across a crowded throne hall without touching a single courtier."*
6. **The Gift / Trojan Horse.** *"The deadliest weapon ever sent to an emperor was a dead friend, brought back to life."*
7. **The Secret Meeting.** *"Four enemies, who hate each other, meet on a cold world to agree on one thing: he must fall."*
8. **The Prophecy Inverted.** *"They spent ten thousand years breeding a messiah. Then he was born too early, to the wrong woman, and he stopped listening."*
9. **The Witness.** Open in an in-universe voice: a chronicler, a survivor or a priest. *"I was there the night the city went white."* Use this rarely, for flagship episodes.
10. **The Myth vs the Man.** *"Trillions pray to him. Statues of him stand on a thousand worlds. He wishes none of it had happened."*

## 4. Re-hook bank (place one at every chapter seam, every 60–90 s)

- "But the conspirators had one problem, and it could see the future."
- "What the Tleilaxu didn't say was what else they had hidden inside him."
- "That should have been the end. It wasn't."
- "He survives the night. His eyes do not."
- "And there was one thing his visions had never shown him."
- "Remember the promise the Tleilaxu made? This is the moment they collect."

**Re-hook rules**
- A re-hook is a *question or a threat about the next chapter*. It is never a summary of the last one.
- End each chapter on a turn: answer the chapter's question, then open the next one.

---

## 5. Visual grammar of the hook (mandatory)

- **Lock picture to word.** When a name, place or object is spoken, it is on screen *on that word*, within ±0.25 s. Cuts are anchored to word timestamps from forced alignment.
- **Shot length.** Cut every 2–4 s in the hook, then 4–7 s in the body.
- **Graphics in the hook:** one stat counter or one name card at most. Save the full motion-graphics vocabulary for the body.
- **Title drop.** Use a transparent animated title card over moving imagery: tracking-in letters, a thin gold rule, and the subtitle.
- **No** on-screen disclaimers or labels in the hook. The publisher adds any labels at upload.

## 6. Hook QA checklist (score it before recording; it must reach 9/10)

- [ ] The first line is under 12 words, concrete, in present tense, and makes no meta-reference.
- [ ] There is a hard number or an irreversible fact by 15 s.
- [ ] An antagonist and a specific weapon are named by 25 s.
- [ ] There is an open loop that is only paid off in the final act.
- [ ] The title drop comes by 45 s, and there is no logo before it.
- [ ] Every hook line has a payoff timestamp in the script.
- [ ] The strongest image of the episode is in the first 3 s.
- [ ] There are no film, release, cast or "explained" references.
- [ ] It works on phone speakers with the music at bed level.
- [ ] Read aloud, it lasts 35–45 s at about 140 wpm.

## 7. Applied: DUN003 hook (v2), archetype 1 combined with archetype 6

> He can see the future. Every war. Every betrayal. Every road that leads away from this throne.
> And on every one of those roads, the galaxy burns.
> Twelve years ago, Paul Atreides won everything. Now sixty-one billion people are dead in his name,
> and four of the most powerful forces in the universe have joined together to destroy him.
> Their weapon is not an army. It is a gift. A dead man, brought back to life.
> And Paul can see the trap. He is going to walk into it anyway.
> This is the story of Dune Messiah. The fall of the man who became a god.

Payoffs:
- "sees the future" → chapter 6, the blind emperor.
- "61 billion" → chapter 2.
- "four forces" → chapter 3.
- "the gift" → chapter 4.
- "walks into the trap" → chapters 5 and 9.

Score: 10/10.
