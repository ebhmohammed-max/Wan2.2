# PART 6AA — IMAGE PROMPT BATCH 042 (`VAL-EP001-IMG0411` – `VAL-EP001-IMG0420`)

**Series:** *The Ancient World of Ice and Fire*
**Episode:** EP001 — *Before the Dragons: The Rise of Old Valyria*
**Batch:** 042 of 105 · `13:40–14:00` · 10 frames · 2 seconds/frame · Ch. IV

**Visual standard:** Photoreal live-action 65mm historical cinema, ARRI Alexa 65, Panavision Primo spherical primes, Kodak Vision3 500T, physically grounded detail, restrained natural film grain, realistic exposure. One palette throughout: **deep indigo and violet night over Qarth**, lit only by two moons, cold stars and sparse pale blue-white lamps.

**Continuity:** *…two moons once hung above the earth, until one wandered too close to the sun and cracked from the heat like a giant egg, spilling a thousand thousand dragons across the heavens after they had drunk the sun's own fire. Maesters of the Citadel understandably dismissed such poetry.*

**The one licensed cosmic image.** This is the only passage in the episode where the frame may leave the physical world, and the narration licenses it explicitly by calling it poetry. The discipline that makes it work is **register, not restraint**: the moons are photographed as *objects* — mottled, dusty, cold, no halo — and the fracture is a **shell crack**, branching and jagged, not an explosion. Nothing blooms, nothing glows, nothing flares.

**Standing directives carried forward, plus three new ones:**
- **`QARTH-MYTHIC` form lock** (from 041): pale carved sandstone, arched colonnades, tiered towers, silk in indigo and violet, deep twilight, pale blue-white lamps, **no gold, no sun, no warm light**, no legible script, Qartheen figures never portraits.
- **NEW — two moons are canon, and only here.** One large silvery-cream with a mottled grey surface; one smaller, duller, bronze-grey. Both pale and cold, no halo, in an indigo sky with cold stars. **They must never appear in any other location's sky.**
- **NEW — the crack is a shell, not a blast.** A single jagged branching fracture running edge to edge, filled with cold white-blue light, the two halves beginning to part with a thin dark seam. Flakes drift outward; they do not fly.
- **NEW — the dragons stay in the voiceover.** The narration promises a thousand thousand dragons across the heavens, but no dragon is shown. The spill is carried by the cracked shell and the shards on the paving. A swarm would break both the silhouette discipline and the cool register.

**Lessons from this batch to carry into 043:**

1. **Myth needs a props department.** `0417` (shell shards on paving) and `0419` (the cracked disc in the hands) do more for the fable than any sky frame, because they put the impossible thing at arm's length where it can be examined. When a passage is unashamedly mythic, give it at least one tactile close-up.
2. **Cold light is the whole trick.** Two moons with no halo, silver and bronze, in indigo — the frame stays photographed rather than illustrated. The instant a halo, flare or glow appears, it becomes a fantasy poster.
3. **A crowd with its back turned is a face, safely.** `0413` and `0414` get scale, awe and *character* out of two dozen people without a single portrait, which is both the Qarthen anonymity rule and the cheapest way to shoot a crowd.
4. **Not showing the dragons was the right call, and it was a judgement, not an omission.** The narration promises a thousand thousand of them; showing a swarm would have broken the silhouette signature, the cool register and the physical-object discipline in one frame. The voiceover carries the hyperbole, the picture carries the evidence.
5. **A reset is survivable if the brief survives.** The briefs are now recorded in this document *before* rendering, so a lost asset is a re-run rather than a re-think. Assets are uploaded the moment they are approved, and the manifest is committed before the upload loop finishes.
6. **Two moons is a canon lock, not a per-frame choice.** Because it now lives in the inventory, no later batch can accidentally put them over Valyria.
