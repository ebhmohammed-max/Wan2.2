**Rerender brief used (resolved):**

1. **`IMG0375` — brown bowl cuts.** The composition, terrain, flock and framing were all correct; the three shepherds had short brown hair. Fixed by stating the hair **positively and specifically** — *"all three have long loose silver-white hair falling past their shoulders"* — and then naming the failures: no brown, dark, auburn or black hair; no bowl cut, no cropped hair, no braids, no headband.

**Lessons from this batch to carry into 039:**

1. **The silhouette signature is now a solved problem.** Four dragon frames across two locations and two very different scales (0371 in open sky, 0378–0380 in the caldera) all came back correct on the first pass because the five descriptors were written into each brief before generation. This is the single most valuable prompt artefact the episode has produced.
2. **Stating an attribute positively beats assuming continuity.** The clan's hair, the location's architecture and the animal's anatomy all drift the moment they live only in the bible. Every frame must re-state what it needs, in the frame's own words.
3. **Two frames can carry an idea with no animal in them at all.** `0372` and `0374` — burnt wool and an arrangement of stones — depict the aftermath of a raid and the birth of systematic observation respectively, and neither can fail anatomically, which makes them the cheapest reliable frames in the episode.
4. **Resting adults need a pose, not a portrait.** Curving the neck back over the animal's own shoulder keeps it legible and heavy while guaranteeing no face is presented to the lens.
5. **At colony scale, keep the juveniles compact and heavy.** Detail added at distance bristles into spikes; mass, neck and head survive.
6. **A hinge batch costs nothing extra.** Changing location mid-batch was seamless because both locations had been fully specified in earlier batches — the work of a hinge batch is continuity, not invention.
