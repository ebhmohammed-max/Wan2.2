**The anatomy failure, precisely.** `0446` is compositionally the best frame of the batch — the cleft converging to a bright blade of sky, the climbing figure tiny against it — and on its first pass it failed on the animal alone: at 2x there were three separate taloned extremities, one gripping the ledge and two hanging. That is a crouched animal that had gained a third leg, the exact failure first recorded at `IMG0398`, and the **third** time this batch's dragon drifted into a crouched, wings-forward, detail-lit pose.

**The pattern is now understood and written into the bible as `9c`.** The count is rarely the problem; **the pose is**. A crouched, detail-lit animal with its wings forward gives the model a gripping extremity it can see and a hanging one it cannot, and it fills both. Countermeasures in order of effectiveness: **(1) put it in flight with both hind legs tucked out of sight; (2) make it a pure silhouette, where the outline enforces the count; (3) state the count numerically and negatively.** `IMG0443` proves the second point better than any argument — a flat black shape with two wings, two planted legs and two horns, and no ambiguity at all.

The re-render brief that fixed `0446`: the animal **in flight, wings spread across the cleft, both hind legs tucked tight against the belly and out of sight**, with `extra limbs, third leg, fourth leg, arms, dangling feet` negated explicitly and the requirement that **zero claws hang below the silhouette**. It worked on the first attempt.

**The horn question, settled rather than re-rendered.** The twin swept-back horns have drifted into a heavier spike crown in `0437` and `0442`. Rather than spend generations chasing it, it is now an **accepted tolerance** in the bible: permitted provided the swept-back direction and one dominant horn pair read clearly and the silhouette is correct. This is a decision recorded in writing, which is the difference between a tolerance and a drift nobody noticed.

**Lessons to carry into 046:**

1. **Pose beats count.** Chasing a limb number in a bad pose is unwinnable; changing the pose makes the number unnecessary. Flight or silhouette, always.
2. **Silhouette is the cheapest compliance.** `0443` cost one generation and holds the anatomy lock perfectly, because a flat shape has nowhere to hide a mistake.
3. **The question is carried by absence.** `0447`–`0450` never show domination — a boot print, dried blood, an open palm, a lowered muzzle, ten paces of empty rock. Nothing is tamed on screen; the audience is left holding the riddle the narration asks.
4. **Detail inserts earn their place.** `0447`'s boot print and hair make an abstract question into evidence you could photograph. One frame like this is worth three of the chamber at scale.
5. **A tolerance must be written down.** Horn drift, palette lushness and costume slips are all acceptances, and an acceptance that is not recorded becomes an invisible inconsistency six batches later.
6. **When the brief is right, it stays right.** Applying `9b` from the first prompt meant zero masonry in ten frames and no wasted generation on a failure this production has now made twice.

**`IMG0446` closed the batch on the second pass.** Re-rendered as the first generation of the next turn, in flight with both hind legs tucked tight to the belly: at 2x there is no third taloned extremity anywhere and nothing hangs below the silhouette. **The countermeasure worked first time, which is the whole argument for ranking fixes instead of trying them at random.**
