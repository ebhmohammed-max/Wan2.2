**Rerender brief used (resolved):**

1. **`IMG0398` — three gripping feet.** At review size the frame looked correct; at 2x the tucked limbs resolved into **three taloned feet** at the base of the animal and the neck had stretched into a swan curve — both hard canon failures. The fix was to make the count explicit and negate the alternatives: *"STRICTLY FOUR LIMBS: exactly TWO muscular taloned hind legs gripping the rock, and exactly TWO wings… no third leg, no fourth leg, no arms, no front legs"*, plus *"a THICK SHORT RINGED NECK (short and thick, not long or curved like a swan)"*. The near wrist-claw on the wing's leading edge is correct book anatomy and was kept.

**Lessons from this batch to carry into 041:**

1. **Heat needs containment to look real.** A single dull seam inside black rock reads as a real volcano; broad glow reads as a video-game lava level. Every warm element in this batch is one low, thin line.
2. **Resting and perched poses are the limb-count danger zone.** Tucked limbs merge. State the count and negate alternatives whenever the animal is sitting, brooding, curling or gripping rock.
3. **Implying the animal is often better than showing it.** A wing shadow crossing a wall, and a head emerging from a cavity, both outrank a full-body frame here — and neither can go wrong anatomically.
4. **A landscape can carry an argument.** `0394` and `0400` say *this place is older and larger than any claim made about it* with no figure, no animal in the centre and no narration on screen.
5. **The myth reads best at the boundary between rock and animal.** `0397` works because the head is half-dissolved into the wall — the answer to "where did dragons come from" staged as a birth out of stone.
6. **Verification at 2x is not optional.** `0398` passed a casual look and failed a zoom. Limb structure, tail count and neck proportion are all small-detail judgements, and small details need magnification.
