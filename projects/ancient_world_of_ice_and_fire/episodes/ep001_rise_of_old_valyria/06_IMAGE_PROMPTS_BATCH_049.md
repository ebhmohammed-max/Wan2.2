**The chapter turns to interrogate its own legend, and the interrogation has no face.** This is the **third** appearance of the Citadel in the episode, and the standing ban has not bent once: **no maester is ever a character.** The disbelief is carried entirely by **ink-stained hands, a forged chain, wet marble and small pools of candlelight**. `0485` crops above the wrists; `0486` renders the maesters strictly from behind; `0487` is the only frame with a face in it, and that face is carved stone.

**Valyria is finally allowed architecture, and it looks grown rather than built.** Dragonflame-fused black stone is the visual signature of Valyrian power: **seamless, mortarless, glassy, continuous, cooled from molten flow, slightly translucent where it thinned.** It must never read as masonry. `0481` — two colossal fused piers leaning together over a fused basalt road with smoke beyond — is the first structure this episode has permitted in Valyria, and it arrives without a mason in sight.

**`IMG0484` is the best single frame of the episode so far, and it is best because of what it does not show.** A 24mm ultra-wide from a high coastal ridge: the Summer Sea on the left, black rock shelves running into white surf, long folds of ash-grey and emerald, three smoking cones inland with their plumes bent west — and on the far coast, **Oldtown as a pale smudge**, hundreds of leagues away, readable only as a shape. Two tiny herdsmen stand on the ridge with their backs to us looking at the water.

**The whole chapter is in that composition.** The distance between a shepherd on a cliff and an institution on the far side of a sea is the distance the narration is asking about — *did the keys of world empire truly pass into shepherd hands?* — and the frame answers nothing. It just shows you how far apart the two things are.

**`IMG0487` deserves a note on judgement, because it is not a perfect frame.** The marble sphinx head at the gate, wet and beaded, chipped at the brow, the folded wing behind it in shallow relief, and the only living thing in frame is the rain. It reads **slightly closer to a classical angel than to a beast-bodied sphinx** — the human half dominates and the lion half is carried only by the clawed forelimb at the lower edge. I accepted it rather than spend a generation: the AFFC reference frame `IMG0035` remains the canonical rendering of the creature, and this is a rain-lit detail of its head at dusk, not a portrait of the species. **A tolerance recorded, not an oversight.**

**`IMG0485` is where the writing ban came closest to breaking, and it held.** A maester's hands on a page that is turned away and out of focus; at 2.4x the text is a blurred block of faint marks with **not one legible word**. That matches the established `0034`/`0037` precedent exactly — the Citadel material has always been allowed the *texture* of script as long as no line can be read — and the chain does the rest of the work: dull iron, copper, bronze and a knot of pale silver, worn bright at the high points by years of wear.

**`IMG0482` is the failure of the batch and the third occurrence of one specific fault.** The two piers flanking the road came back as **stacked rubble blocks with visible joint lines and pitted mortar texture** — coursed masonry, which is the one thing dragonflame-fused stone can never look like. The composition was right, the shepherd's back and loose silver hair were right, and the stone was wrong. This is `0453` again, and it confirms the rule: **the anti-masonry negation list is not optional in any frame where Valyria builds anything.** The re-render brief now negates *ashlar, coursed masonry, stacked blocks, joints, mortar, rubble, brick, dry-stone, pitted masonry* and demands a surface that looks **poured or melted and then cooled, slightly translucent at thin edges, like obsidian**.

**The last three frames finished the Citadel material, and each one solved the same problem a different way.** `0488` makes the argument **literally**: a bare limestone plinth in a dark vault with four empty, slightly rusted **bolt holes** where a statue's feet were once fixed, and no statue on it. Nothing was left behind to look at. `0489` gives the institution its exterior — squat, weathered, administrative, narrow windows, no ornament, no banners, no gilding, dissolving upward into raincloud — and **not one figure in it resolves**, which I checked at 5x: the smudges on the bridge parapet stay paper-cut silhouettes. `0490` closes the batch from the Citadel's own tallest tower at night: the city as a dark field of tiles punctuated by **hundreds of small warm lamps**, an ordinary evening rather than a festival, the Honeywine black, and the Whispering Sound running flat to a thin pale horizon.

**`0490` is the sceptic's own case, seen from his own window.** A small warm civilisation at the edge of a very large dark — and the frame does not editorialise. It simply puts you where Yandel stands.

**`0482` is the batch's proof of method.** It failed on the first pass with coursed rubble and joint lines, exactly as `0453` had, and it was fixed on the second pass **by naming absences rather than describing the material harder**: no ashlar, no coursed masonry, no stacked blocks, no joint lines, no mortar, no rubble, no brick, no dry-stone, no pitting, no capital, no base, no cornice, no ornament — plus "one single cooled mass" and "slightly translucent at the edge." What came back is genuinely **poured**: glassy, continuous, jointless, faintly green where the light grazes the thin edge. **Three failures produced one sentence, and the sentence works.**

**Lessons to carry into 050:**

1. **A negation list is a load-bearing part of the prompt, not a safety net.** Three failures (`0286`, `0453`, `0482`) have come from describing a material and trusting the positive. Every architectural frame from here gets the absences spelled out.
2. **The strongest composition can be the emptiest one.** `0484` works because two tiny figures and one pale smudge carry an entire argument across a sea. Nothing is explained and nothing needs to be.
3. **Institutional authority is carried by hands and metal, never by a face.** Five Citadel appearances into the production, the ban has produced better frames than a portrait would have.
4. **Blur is an honest solution to a forbidden subject.** A page out of focus is not a cheat — it is what a scholar's desk actually looks like to a camera at 50mm, and it satisfies the writing ban without any visible evasion.
5. **Record the near-misses.** The sphinx's angelic lean is the kind of thing that becomes an inconsistency six batches later if nobody writes it down.
6. **Check material, not just anatomy.** The 2x pass caught `0482`'s joints; the 3x pass caught `0476`'s six limbs. **Both failures were invisible at 950px.**
