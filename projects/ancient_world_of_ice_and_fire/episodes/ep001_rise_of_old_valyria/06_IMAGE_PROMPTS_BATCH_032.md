**Standing directive for `IMG0316` and all later dragon frames — the dragon must be VISIBLE.** The establishing frame is not a hidden-animal frame. `DRG-VAL-001` is shown dominant, legible and monumental; the "fragments" language in the director's note governs *pose* (no hero shot, no camera contact, no fire breath), not *legibility*. Do not solve an anatomy failure by cropping the animal out of frame or pushing it into darkness — that was tried on passes 4–6 of `IMG0316` and produced a plank, a black frame and a bat silhouette.

**The `IMG0316` arc — seven generations, and what actually fixed it.** Pass 1 read six-limbed with a long cloak and a bald crown. Pass 2 fixed the legs but kept the cloak and crown. Pass 3 corrected the elder and the limbs, then the folded wing wrist rendered as a pointed **golden beak**. Pass 4 tried a cropped upper-right flank — it produced two **eye sockets** in the wing mass, worse than the beak because a genuinely wing-like shape read as a face. Pass 5 pushed the animal into darkness as a thin lit band — the band became a flat **plank or railing** and the frame crashed to a mean luma of 0.06. Pass 6 went backlit-membrane — the membrane finally read as leather, but brought a literal **pool of fire** and a fully spread bat wing. Pass 7 combined the correct elements: monumental scale, **neck terminating above the frame** rather than in frame, one folded wing as a taut ragged membrane, blunt-clawed forefoot on black rock, ground black to the corners.

**Pass-one-through-seven lessons to carry into Batch 033:**

1. **Stating the limb count is not enough** — the *ground contact* has to be described (two taloned feet plus two wing-knuckles, nothing else), or the wing-shoulder framing leaves room for a phantom third leg.
2. **"Embedded, not resting on" needs a material model.** "Bones fused into glass" rendered bones lying on a slab three times running; "thick glass has flowed over the bones and set hard, each bone locked under the surface with only its upper face exposed, no contact shadow beneath it" finally worked. Value matters as much as geometry: bright white bone also read as clean and new, so the bone had to be described as dirty tan-grey, close in value to the slab.
3. **A closed animal mass in frame will resolve as a head.** This is the governing lesson of the batch. A pointed wing-wrist became a beak; a cropped convex flank became eye sockets. Negating "beak / horn / eye / face" pulls the whole head concept *in* — four passes proved it. The reliable fix is compositional: **run the neck or shoulder out of the frame edge**, so the mass has no terminus and no closed contour. Removal of the terminus, not negation of the feature.
4. **Do not hide the dragon to avoid anatomy errors.** See the standing directive above. Crushing the frame to near-black or reducing the animal to a lit strip trades a local error for a global one: an illegible frame that no longer does its narrative job. Fix value and shape at full legibility instead.
5. **"Magma-lit" produces fire.** Even with "no fire" negated, a warm wall behind the animal rendered as a literal column of flame; the acceptable language is dull ember-red heat *inside* the rock, evenly diffuse, with the throat unlit. Pass 6 of `IMG0316` repeated this as a literal pool of fire beneath the wing.
6. **Columnar basalt drifts to masonry from any level ledge.** Angled slabs, snapped plates and irregular shattered blocks survive; horizontal flat-topped ledges do not.
7. **Pale mineral crust still reads as snow** under cold grey key (ninth occurrence); ground must be described as pure matte black wet basalt with black glass shards.
8. **The elder's crown must be written as continuous hair in the subject text**, not merely as "no baldness" in the negatives — and it is worth a 500% crop before approval, because a small pale scalp patch reads as a shaved patch at one-fourth scale.
