**Continuity:** *…that the Valyrians did not discover the art of taming dragons on their own — because dragons had already been mastered ages earlier in the Shadow beyond Asshai. According to this Asshai'i tradition, a people so ancient that surviving memory preserves neither their name nor their unmasked faces first bent dragons…*

**This is the most dangerous line in the episode, and the batch is built around that danger.** *Unmasked faces* invites a face; a face invites a character; a character would make the Shadow a nation with a costume and an actor. The whole point of the claim is that **these people cannot be pictured.** So the camera is never placed anywhere a face could exist: distant silhouettes, backs of heads, hoods hanging past the crown. Every Shadow prompt negates *face, mask, eyes, mouth, nose, profile* by name. **The anonymity is the subject, not a workaround for it.**

**The batch also crosses out of Asshai entirely.** For five frames we are still in the archive; from `0466` the film is in **the Shadow beyond Asshai**, which carries the strictest design rule in the production so far: **it must never look built.** Raw sheared rock, glassy in patches, scored as if by heat, totally untooled. No doorway, no wall, no stair, no statue, no idol, no carved ornament. Nothing there has a maker, because the claim is that nobody remembers who they were.

**`IMG0462` is the best idea in the batch, and it cost one generation.** An 85mm macro of a black stone shelf edge where generations of hands have hollowed a shallow depression. Nothing is cut into it, nothing is written on it, no name is scratched anywhere — and it is a more moving record of an anonymous people than any portrait could be, because **wear is the one kind of evidence that survives when identity does not.** If the episode has an argument about how the Shadow is remembered, this frame makes it physically.

**`IMG0463` is the mask rule solved, and it is worth describing precisely.** The figure is close — intimate, in a stone niche — and the head is bowed so steeply that the coarse hood falls past the crown, so the entire head is cloth and shadow. At 2x there is **no eye, no nose, no mouth and no mask plane**; a few strands of fine grey hair at the hood's edge are the only human detail, and they are what make the void feel like a person rather than a costume. **A bowed hood is a stronger anonymity device than a hidden face**, because a hidden face still has a shape.

**`IMG0464` clears the writing ban at the place it was most likely to fail.** The end of a closed scroll, cord knotted twice and still tied, and a wax seal **verified blank at 4x** — no stamp, no impression, no symbol. A seal is the one object in this library that instinctively wants an emblem on it, and the brief said plainly: *a plain, blank, thumb-pressed lump of dull dark red wax with no impression.* It held.

**`IMG0467` and `IMG0468` are the answer to *unmasked faces* — the people are too far away to have one.** `0467` puts three hooded silhouettes in a line at the far end of the pass, their number the only information in frame. `0468` spaces four figures across a bare ash floor beneath sheared cliffs, and the scale does the argument: **a people this old are not characters, they are a distance.** Neither frame has a face, and neither frame misses one.

**Lessons to carry into 048:**

1. **When the script says "no face", move the camera, don't cover the face.** Distance, backs and bowed hoods beat veils, shadows and masks every time. A covered face is still a face-shaped hole in the frame.
2. **The strongest evidence of anonymous people is wear.** `0462`'s hollowed shelf edge says more than a portrait because it is the trace of hands rather than the hands themselves.
3. **Take the reading rule to the object, at maximum magnification.** `0464`'s blank seal was checked at 4x, where a stamped emblem would have been obvious. Seals, coins, signets and stamps are where writing sneaks back in.
4. **An unbuilt landscape needs negatives, not adjectives.** "Cyclopean" produced a Roman temple in `0453`; the fix was a named list of absences. The Shadow gets the same treatment: no doorway, no wall, no stair, no statue, no idol — because a model will build something if you only tell it the rock is huge.
5. **Scale is an argument.** `0468` is not an establishing shot, it is the claim that these people are older than memory — and the only way to photograph that is to make them small.
6. **Cross the location boundary deliberately.** The stale scene-breakdown refs still tag the Shadow frames `LOC-VAL-001`; the narration slice, not the ref, decides where the camera is. Third time this has mattered.

**`IMG0470` closed the batch and it is the frame the chapter was withholding.** A 50mm in the Shadow: the dragon's head and neck bent low, muzzle almost on the rock, twin horns flat to the skull, one lowered brass-gold eye, a thread of black smoke from the nostril — and a bare pale forearm entering from the left with the hand resting flat on the muzzle, the figure's hood a soft black mass in the foreground. **No face in the frame and none possible.** The composition solves the limb-lock by cropping: only a head and a neck are in shot, so there is nothing to miscount. That is the silhouette lesson turned into a framing strategy, and it is the cheapest version of the fix yet.
