## The rule for this batch: Barth's sentence, not Barth's fantasy

The narration finally says out loud what six batches of images have only implied:

> *“Valyrian bloodmages had delved into forbidden secrets of flesh, mating or altering creatures of fire and tying dragons to human bloodlines.”*

This is the most dangerous batch in the chapter, and the danger is not gore — it is **literalisation**. A phrase like *“mating or altering creatures of fire”* invites a creature in a pen, a harness, a cage, a body on a slab. The moment any of that is shown, the episode stops being a documentary about evidence and becomes the lurid heresy the maesters denounced. It would also be the first genuinely unusable footage in the production.

**So the batch is built on one reading of bible §4h:** Septon Barth never saw Valyria. What we are given are **his categories**, and the images must show *the shape of a claim made at a distance* — never a depicted act. Three devices, used in rotation:

1. **Residue instead of event.** What a practice left behind (ash, stain, wear, a tool, an empty space) rather than the practice.
2. **The category as a specimen.** Objects separated out and labelled by their own material and weight: scale, horn, bone, stone, ash.
3. **The boundary, not the act.** A threshold, a door, a wall, a cord — the point past which Barth is guessing.

**Hard bans for all ten frames:** no mating, no coupling, no human–creature hybrid, no pregnant body, no cage, no pen, no harness, no collar, no chain, no muzzle, no restraint, no creature in distress, no blood, no wound, no scalpel, no operating surface, no figures in ritual with a creature between them. **Where dragons appear at all, they appear dead, distant, geological or partial** — a tooth, a heap of bone, a shape on the horizon — never alive in the same frame as a person.

**Status of the frames:** all ten `APPROVED` (ten first passes, zero rejections).
