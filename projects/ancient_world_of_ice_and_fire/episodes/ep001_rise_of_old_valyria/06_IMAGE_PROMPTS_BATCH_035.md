**Rerender briefs that were used (all resolved):**

1. **`IMG0341` — upright demon.** Pass 2 failed because the body's long axis pointed at the camera, stacking the near limbs vertically; the fix was to run the body **diagonally out of the top-left corner**. Pass 3 fixed the axis but draped the wing across the chest as a convex dome, which read as a head and shoulders; the fix was to hold the wing **high and edge-on as a thin flat blade**. Negatives added: "standing upright, bipedal, rearing, cape, cloak, hooded figure, dome, bulge, convex closed shape".
2. **`IMG0345` — the head found the lens, and the hide went yellow.** Profile with the muzzle pointing away and the eye not visible from that side, plus an explicit colour lock: "basalt-black oxidised plate, charcoal, iron-grey — no olive, no yellow, no green, no ochre on the creature's hide".

**Lessons from this batch to carry into 036:**

1. **A low angle plus a head-on body axis produces a biped.** Third occurrence of the same failure across two batches (horse, demon, demon). The fix is always the camera axis, never the prose.
2. **Any closed convex mass inside a silhouette resolves as a head.** This is the Batch 032 rule and it does not care what the mass is made of — a wing membrane bulging across a chest reads as a skull and shoulders exactly like a cropped flank did. The cure is to flatten the plane, not to negate "head".
3. **Sulphur yellow leaks into the creature.** Three times now. The negative has to name the hue explicitly, every time the palette is warm.
4. **The membrane macro is the safest frame type in the episode.** Four of four passed first time across two batches, because with no head in frame there is no head to invent.
5. **Internal heat works at scale as long as the plates stay black.** `0346` glows densely and still reads as heat *inside* stone-flesh; a continuous lit sheet would have broken the rule.
6. **Wingspan is a silhouette problem, not a detail problem.** `0349` sold 200 feet at 24mm from far away with enormous black negative space, not from any close view of the wing.
