**Three rerender briefs, queued behind the 10-images-per-turn cap:**

1. **`IMG0321` — the cavern is lit like a furnace.** The walking figure is correct; keep him exactly as he is. Replace the background: the chamber is almost entirely black, walls unlit, only thin grey steam and a few hairline ember cracks in the floor. **No animal in frame at all** — this frame is the last one before the reveal and must not spend it.
2. **`IMG0322` — he met the lens.** Keep the compression and the shallow falloff, but shoot him from behind and slightly to his left, head tipped down and away, so only the line of the jaw and the soot on the cheek read. No eye, no iris, no readable face.
3. **`IMG0323` — the distant animal grew feathers and a pale ledge.** Keep the crouch at the rim. The far coil must be one low unbroken dark mass with no splayed plates, no light-catching edges and no fan geometry; the ledge must be black rock with dull ember only in hairlines.

**Lessons from this batch to carry into 034:**

1. **A warm lit background is a fire, always.** After four batches, "magma-lit" still resolves as a lit room or a burning wall. Write the darkness first and the heat as hairline cracks in the floor, and negate "lit chamber, glowing wall, furnace, backlit doorway" explicitly.
2. **Telephoto + a human in three-quarter turn will find the lens.** At 65mm and under, any profile that shows two eyes breaks the gaze discipline. Write "from behind and slightly to his left, head tipped down and away, eyes fully hidden, no readable face" as the subject, not as a negative.
3. **A distant animal with light-catching plates grows feathers.** At small scale, ribbed pale-edged plates read as shafts. Distant animals must be one unbroken dark silhouette with a matte surface.
4. **Warm rock drifts to ochre and then to sandstone.** The ledge under an animal must be black; if the ember light lands on the rock surface it stops being an underlight and becomes coloured stone.
5. **The whole-animal reveal is achievable in one pass** once the head may be present: describe the skull positively (heavy wedge, blunt crocodilian snout, twin swept-back horns, eyes shut) rather than relying on a wing fragment to imply the animal. 0324 is the proof.
6. **The healed wing tear is now a continuity prop.** Writing it as a pale seam between plates worked in both the cowled wing and the macro; carry the phrase forward verbatim.
